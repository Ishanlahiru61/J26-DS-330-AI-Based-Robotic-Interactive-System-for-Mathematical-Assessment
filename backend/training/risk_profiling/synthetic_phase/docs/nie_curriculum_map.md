### What changes from grade to grade (consequences for the item bank)

| Skill | Grade 1 | Grade 2 | Grade 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counting objects | to 20 | to 100; in 2s | to 100 in 2s, 5s, 10s |
| Numerals read/write | 1–9 | to 100, with zero | to 1000 |
| **Non-symbolic** comparison (which set has more) | ✅ by one-to-one matching | (assumed) | (assumed) |
| **Symbolic** comparison (which number is bigger) | ❌ **not taught** | ✅ < 100 | ✅ ≤ 999 |
| Place value | ❌ | ✅ tens/ones | ✅ hundreds |
| Addition | sum ≤ 9 with objects | ≤ 20, then ≤ 99 no carry | 3-digit, one carry |
| Subtraction | from ≤ 9, objects only | ≤ 20, then ≤ 99 no borrow | ≤ 99 no borrow |
| Multiplication / division | ❌ | ❌ | × 2, 5, 10; ÷ 2 |

Two direct consequences:
1. A **symbolic number comparison** item ("which is bigger, 7 or 4?") is **not** a valid Grade 1 item. It
   is curriculum content from Grade 2. In Grade 1, number sense must be tested **non-symbolically** (cubes).
2. **Multiplication and division** items are valid only from Grade 3. Two of Component 2's seven skills
   (multiplication/grouping, division/sharing) must therefore be switched off for Grades 1–2.

Measurement, time, money and shapes appear in all three grades but stay out of scope (see Section 1).

Measurement, time, money, shapes, direction, symmetry and data (competencies 9–17) are in the
syllabus but are **outside the scope** of this component, because the dyscalculia evidence base
(evidence register, Step D) covers number processing and arithmetic, not those strands.

## 3. The NIE three-level achievement scale

The guide's assessment report sheet (pp. 121–122) rates each competency on three levels. The
**same three levels** are used on the Grade 1 sheet ([NIE-G1] p. 80) and the Grade 2 sheet ([NIE-G2] p. 113):

| NIE level (Sinhala) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| සාර්ථකව කරයි | Does it successfully |
| දුෂ්කරතා පෙන්නුම් කරයි | Shows difficulty |
| තවදුරටත් උදව් අවශ්‍යයි | Needs further help |

**How we use it:** as the official vocabulary for **curriculum mastery per competency**, which the
teacher report can show.

**How we do not use it:** it is an achievement rating, not a clinical risk level. "Needs further
help" in one competency does not mean dyscalculia risk. Risk comes from the evidence-weighted,
age-referenced formula (Steps E–F), and must persist across sessions (DSM-5 Criterion A).

## 4. Consequences for the question bank (to agree with the question-generation component)

1. Questions for a Grade 3 child should stay **inside** the ranges above (for example, comparison up to 999, addition with only one carry).
   A wrong answer outside the taught range is not evidence of difficulty.
2. Each question should carry the **NIE competency code** (for example `2.4`), so every result can be traced to the curriculum.
3. Multiplication by 2, 5, 10 and division by 2 **are** Grade 3 content. They are kept, inside the "arithmetic" type, but only at this level.
